## Processing

Each was fetched at 2000px wide and re-encoded with mozjpeg at quality 80.
Hero frames are 1800x1013 (16:9), capability frames 1040x780 (4:3), and the
auth panel 900x1400 (portrait). All were cropped, never squeezed.

Crops used sharp's attention strategy — it frames the most salient region, so
a tile never lands on an empty corner — **except** where that strategy chose
badly and a centre crop was used instead. Re-crop by eye rather than trusting
the strategy blind; it was wrong twice in the sibling CRM set.

## Why these frames and not prettier ones

The brief was a Kenyan setting, and the first pass was not: a European
warehouse aisle, a boardroom of people who look nothing like the people who
will use this. Stock libraries make that easy to do by accident, because the
default result for "warehouse" or "accountant" is Western. Every frame here
was re-sourced against that, and the ones that stayed generic — a hand
signing a form, a file changing hands — stayed because they are hands and a
document, which belong to nobody in particular.

Searching "african <thing>" on Pexels returns a pool of African *people*
rather than African *workplaces*, so the same handful of portraits come back
for "warehouse", "engineer" and "call centre" alike. Searching for the work
("black man warehouse boxes", "african engineer helmet") is what actually
found the pictures of the job being done.

Nothing here carries legible third-party branding, a burnt-in timestamp, or a
foreign banknote. That last one matters: this product prices in shillings, and
a stock photograph of US dollars on an invoice quietly says otherwise.
